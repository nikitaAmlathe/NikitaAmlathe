Operational Learnings
Supply chain observations from 9+ years of ground-level execution
Planning vs. Execution Alignment Inventory Governance
One of the most consistent gaps in supply chain operations is the assumption that a well-designed strategy will automatically translate to ground execution. Inventory SOPs, material flows, and replenishment plans only deliver reliable results when they are designed with a clear understanding of physical constraints — dock space, workforce capability, SAP transaction accuracy, and actual material movement patterns. Strategies built without this ground context consistently produce exceptions that require manual workarounds.
Inventory Accuracy as Daily Discipline Process Control
Stock accuracy at 100% is not a milestone achieved during audit preparation — it is maintained through daily cycle counts, consistent FIFO enforcement, and SAP balances that are always validated with physical reality through regular reconciliation processes. Organisations that treat inventory accuracy as a monthly review activity will consistently find discrepancies. Those that build it into daily operational rhythm will remain audit-ready at all times.
GRN TAT as an Operational Lever Execution Efficiency
Inbound processing speed is an underutilised supply chain efficiency lever. Most attention goes to outbound SLAs, but unresolved GRN delays create staging congestion, demurrage exposure, and production material shortfalls. Redesigning staging flows and standardising dock-to-GRN procedures can reduce processing time significantly without additional headcount or system changes.
Vendor Governance over Rate Negotiation Supply Chain Coordination
Managing 60+ logistics vendors across three states clarified one important distinction: sustainable cost control comes from structured performance governance, not from periodic rate pressure. Vendors respond to clear performance metrics, consistent feedback mechanisms, and structured RFQ processes far more reliably than to informal negotiations. Performance-linked contracts combined with route-level data create a more stable and cost-efficient vendor ecosystem over time.
